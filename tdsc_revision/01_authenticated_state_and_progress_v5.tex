\section{System Model and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:model}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Replicas, Network, and Consensus Substrate}

AUTIG runs among a static set of replicas
$\mathcal R=\{r_1,\ldots,r_n\}$, of which at most $f$ are Byzantine.  A
replica that is not Byzantine is \emph{correct}.  One replica acts as the
order leader and proposes fair-order fragments; the remaining replicas verify
each proposal before participating in the underlying Byzantine fault-tolerant
(BFT) protocol.  The BFT protocol totally orders accepted fragments or their
data-available digests and provides agreement and finality.  AUTIG does not
replace this agreement layer.

The network is partially synchronous~\cite{dwork1988consensus}.  Before an
unknown global stabilization time (GST), messages may be delayed arbitrarily.
After GST, a message sent between correct replicas is delivered within the
network-delay bound $\Delta$.  Safety and order-fairness hold independently of
message delay.  Progress additionally assumes that the hosting BFT eventually
installs a correct order leader and commits valid fragment proposals.

Clients may be Byzantine and may submit conflicting, duplicated, or
strategically timed transactions.  A transaction is \emph{valid} if it passes
the deterministic admission predicate of the replicated application.  The
fairness and progress statements below apply only after the full transaction
contents needed by that predicate are available; an identifier without
available contents cannot become executable merely by appearing in ordering
evidence.  AUTIG uses content-bound identifiers: for canonical transaction
contents $\mathsf{tx}_u$, identifier $u$ includes
$\Commit(\mathsf{tx}_u;\mathsf{AUTIG/transaction})$.  Applications with a
separate native identifier bind that identifier to the same content digest.

\subsection{Adversary and Cryptographic Assumptions}

The adversary is computationally bounded and may adaptively corrupt up to $f$
replicas in total.  Corruptions are non-mobile: once corrupted, a replica
remains Byzantine.  Byzantine replicas may equivocate, omit or delay their messages,
report arbitrary receipt orders, and collude with Byzantine clients.  The
adversary also schedules network delivery subject to partial synchrony after
GST.  It cannot forge a correct replica's signature, find collisions in the
hash function, or cause the underlying BFT to commit two conflicting fragment
histories.

Every replica has a certified signing key.  Signatures are existentially
unforgeable under chosen-message attack, and $H$ is collision resistant.  We
write
\[
 \Commit(X;\mathsf{dom})
   =H(\mathsf{dom}\parallel\Enc(X))
\]
for a domain-separated commitment to the canonical encoding of $X$.  All
sets, maps, trees, and fragment fields have deterministic encodings.

\subsection{Fairness Objective and Integer Thresholds}
\label{sec:fairness-objective}

Following Themis~\cite{kelkar2023themis}, $\gamma\in(1/2,1]$
denotes a fraction of \emph{honest} replicas.  Define
\[
 q_h=\left\lceil\gamma(n-f)\right\rceil.
\]
Let the committed output be partitioned into consecutive fairness batches, and
write $b(u)$ for the batch containing transaction $u$.  AUTIG satisfies
$\gamma$-batch-order-fairness if, for every pair of distinct valid transactions
$x$ and $y$ that are eventually received by all correct replicas,
\[
 \left|\{i\in\mathcal R_C:x\prec_i y\}\right|\ge q_h
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b(x)\le b(y)
 \tag{1}\label{eq:batch-order-fairness}
\]
whenever $y$ is finalized.  The relation $x\prec_i y$ refers to replica $i$'s
actual first-receipt order.  Transactions in one strongly connected component
(SCC) share a fairness-batch index; their deterministic execution
serialization does not strengthen the property in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:batch-order-fairness}.

AUTIG uses the integer thresholds
\[
 T_{\mathrm{solid}}=n-2f,
 \qquad
 T_{\mathrm{non\mbox{-}blank}}=T_{\mathrm{edge}}
   =n-q_h+1.
 \tag{2}\label{eq:integer-thresholds}
\]
The feasibility condition needed by the fairness proof is
\[
 2q_h\ge n+2f+1.
 \tag{3}\label{eq:integer-feasibility}
\]
When $\gamma(n-f)$ is integral, Eq.~\eqref{eq:integer-thresholds}
gives $T_{\mathrm{edge}}=n(1-\gamma)+\gamma f+1$, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:integer-feasibility} is equivalent to
\[
 n>\frac{2f(\gamma+1)}{2\gamma-1}.
\]
The integer forms in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:integer-thresholds}--
\eqref{eq:integer-feasibility} govern non-integral parameter settings.  If
$\gamma_{\mathrm{all}}$ instead denotes the fraction of all replicas, the two
parameterizations are related by
\[
 \gamma=\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{all}}n-f}{n-f},
 \qquad
 \gamma_{\mathrm{all}}=\frac{\gamma(n-f)+f}{n}.
 \tag{4}\label{eq:gamma-reparameterization}
\]

\subsection{Graph Preliminaries and Protocol Scope}

For a directed graph $G=(V,E)$, $\mathsf{Anc}_G(u)$ contains $u$ and every
vertex with a directed path to $u$.  An SCC is a maximal set of mutually
reachable vertices.  Contracting every SCC yields the acyclic condensation
graph.  A set $X\subseteq V$ is \emph{down-closed} if
$u\in X$ implies $\mathsf{Anc}_G(u)\subseteq X$.

AUTIG separates safety from progress.  Its safety objective combines agreement
from the underlying BFT, deterministic authenticated-state transitions, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:batch-order-fairness}.  Its progress result is conditional:
under eventual evidence admission and a correct order leader, a transaction is
eventually finalized once its live ancestor set stops expanding.  The progress
model therefore excludes executions in which an adversary extends the live
ancestor set indefinitely.  Section~\ref{sec:discussion-scope} discusses this
scope.

\section{The AUTIG Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Protocol Overview}
\label{sec:protocol-overview}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AUTIG separates authenticated evidence replication from graph materialization.
Every replica deterministically applies committed, prefix-consistent receipt
logs and maintains the resulting live positions and pair weights.  The order
leader additionally materializes these values as the persistent UTIG,
incrementally updates affected edges, and computes a finalizable subgraph.
Followers remain graph-free: they evaluate pair predicates from their local
evidence state and verify a structural certificate instead of maintaining the
UTIG edge set, SCC decomposition, or condensation DAG.

Each protocol round has five steps.  Replicas first extend their signed
observation logs.  The order leader then applies the admitted extensions to
the committed evidence state, refreshes the affected UTIG region, extracts a
safe down-closed transaction set, and attaches a certificate for its SCC
partition and boundary.  Followers replay the same evidence transition and
verify the certificate against locally derived weights.  Verification creates
a candidate post-state; the candidate becomes visible to subsequent rounds
only after the underlying BFT commits the corresponding fragment.  A
checkpoint commits the complete live-evidence state required to resume this
process after recovery or order-leader handoff.

Safe extraction accounts for evidence that has not yet appeared in committed
log prefixes.  A transaction is finalizable only after it is Solid and every
one of its graph ancestors is also Solid.  Consequently, the fairness
antecedent for every finalized transaction has enough committed visibility to
form the required dependency edge.  A blocker forest certifies why each
excluded Solid transaction is not yet safe, while a complete incoming-frontier
check proves that the proposed set is down-closed.  This design preserves the
asymmetry targeted by AUTIG: followers replicate authenticated evidence but
avoid persistent full-graph materialization and full-live-graph extraction.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Prefix-Consistent LocalOrders}
\label{sec:prefix-consistent-localorders}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AUTIG authenticates cumulative ordering evidence through committed,
prefix-consistent observation logs.  Replica $i$ maintains an append-only log
$O_i[1],O_i[2],\ldots$ containing transaction identifiers in first-receipt
order.  An honest replica appends each identifier once and reports only
contiguous extensions of this log.  Thus, if an honest replica reports $v$ at
position $b$, every transaction that it received before $v$ occurs at a
position smaller than $b$ in the same committed prefix.  This
\emph{prefix-completeness} rule connects admitted evidence to the
receive-order premise of batch-order-fairness.

Log validation and contribution to the current live-evidence state are
distinct.  A transaction may be finalized after an honest replica appends it
locally but before the corresponding extension is committed.  Such a delayed
extension remains admissible: the finalized identifier is needed to validate
sequence and hash-chain continuity, but it does not re-enter the live set and
does not create new incident pair evidence.  Repeated occurrences of an
already finalized identifier are likewise continuity-only and are ignored by
the live-evidence transition.  If retained committed log history exposes two
first positions for the same identifier, the two signed extensions are
evidence of equivocation; safety does not rely on detecting such repetition
after the identifier has been finalized.

For fragment sequence $r$, replica $i$ signs
\[
 L_i^r=\langle \mathsf{epoch}_r,r,i,\ell_i^{-},\ell_i^{+},
 \eta_i^{-},\mathsf{chunk}_i^r,\eta_i^{+},\sigma_i\rangle.
\]
Here, $\mathsf{chunk}_i^r$ contains the identifiers at the contiguous local
positions $\ell_i^{-}+1,\ldots,\ell_i^{+}$,
$\eta_i^{-}$ is the preceding committed log head, and
\[
 \eta_i^{+}=\Commit(\eta_i^{-},\ell_i^{-},\ell_i^{+},
              \mathsf{chunk}_i^r;\mathsf{AUTIG/local\mbox{-}log}).
\]
An empty extension has $\ell_i^{+}=\ell_i^{-}$ and
$\eta_i^{+}=\eta_i^{-}$.  The parameter $lo\text{-}size$ limits the number of
identifiers in one contiguous extension; it is a transport chunk size, not a
sliding window that may skip earlier log positions.

Let $\mathsf{Seq}_{r-1}[i]$ and $\mathsf{Head}_{r-1}[i]$ be the position and
log head installed for $i$ by the preceding committed fragment.  We write
$\mathcal L_r\equiv\mathcal L_{\mathrm{batch}}$ for the admitted batch in
fragment $r$.  A LocalOrder
is admissible only if its signature, epoch, sender, and round are valid,
$\ell_i^{-}=\mathsf{Seq}_{r-1}[i]$,
$\eta_i^{-}=\mathsf{Head}_{r-1}[i]$, its positions are contiguous, its
identifiers are distinct within the extension, and every listed live
identifier has no earlier live position for $i$.  Already finalized
identifiers are not grounds for rejecting an otherwise valid extension.  A
batch $\mathcal L_r$ contains exactly $n-f$ admissible LocalOrders from
distinct senders.  These rules make replay idempotent and make conflicting
extensions of the same committed prefix public evidence of replica
equivocation.

A LocalOrder names transactions by identifier, while the candidate fragment
or the hosting BFT data-availability layer supplies a digest-bound reference to
the corresponding contents.  Before assigning a first live position, every
follower requires those contents to be available and to satisfy the
application's deterministic validity predicate.  A missing or invalid
transaction rejects the candidate transition; continuity-only identifiers in
$\mathsf{Done}_{r-1}$ need no retransmitted contents.  The canonical content
commitment is retained as $\mathsf{TxRef}_r[u]$ while $u$ is live; storage
locators are local and are not part of consensus state.  The hosting BFT's
data-availability mechanism makes the committed bytes retrievable.  Repeated
evidence for $u$ must resolve to the same content commitment.

Transaction liveness additionally requires eventual admission of pending
honest extensions.  Correct replicas continuously retransmit their next
uncommitted chunk and never emit an empty chunk while a log position is
pending.  After GST, an honest order leader collects admissible extensions for
the protocol collection window and selects $n-f$ senders according to a
round-rotating public priority; for replica identifiers in
$\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$, we use
$\mathsf{prio}_r(i)=(i-r)\bmod n$.  If fewer than $n-f$ extensions are
available, the leader does not form a fragment and the timeout mechanism
advances the order-leader epoch.  We assume that the hosting BFT eventually
installs an honest order leader and that a valid transaction reported by a
correct replica is eventually disseminated, with its contents, to all correct
replicas.  Under these conditions, every finite position in a correct
replica's append-only log is eventually admitted even when $lo\text{-}size$
is bounded.  Safety is independent of the sender selection; the rotating rule
is used only for transaction liveness.

The state transition below uses the honest-replica parameterization and exact
integer thresholds fixed in Section~\ref{sec:fairness-objective}; the
protocol never reinterprets $\gamma$ as a fraction of all replicas.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Authenticated Graph-Free Follower State}
\label{sec:authenticated-evidence-state}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For each live transaction $u$, let
\[
 \mathsf{Pos}_r(i,u)\in\mathbb N\cup\{\bot\}
\]
be the unique position at which replica $i$ first committedly reports $u$.
The visibility indicator and cumulative pair weight are derived as
\[
 X_r(i,u)=\mathbf 1[\mathsf{Pos}_r(i,u)\ne\bot],
\]
\[
 W_r(u,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
 \mathbf 1[\mathsf{Pos}_r(i,u)\ne\bot\land
            \mathsf{Pos}_r(i,v)\ne\bot\land
            \mathsf{Pos}_r(i,u)<\mathsf{Pos}_r(i,v)].
 \tag{5}\label{eq:position-weight}
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:position-weight} gives every replica at most one lifetime
contribution to a live unordered pair, so $0\le W_r(u,v)\le n$.  In
particular, retransmitting a LocalOrder cannot amplify a replica's vote.
Followers may maintain $W_r$ incrementally as a cache, but the per-replica
positions are the authoritative evidence from which the cache is uniquely
derived.

Let $\mathsf{Live}_r$ contain every reported but unfinalized transaction and
define
\[
 c_r(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_r(i,u),\qquad
 S_r(u)=\textsc{DetermineState}(c_r(u)).
\]
Because positions are assigned once, $c_r(u)$ is monotone while $u$ remains
live.  Hence a transaction moves only
$\Blank\rightarrow\Shaded\rightarrow\Solid$; absence from a later
$\mathcal L_r$ supplies no evidence and never resets its state.  The
authenticated active set is
\[
 A_r=\{u\in\mathsf{Live}_r:S_r(u)\ne\Blank\}.
 \tag{6}\label{eq:active-set}
\]
We retain $B_r$ for the non-Blank set induced by the current batch alone;
cumulative extraction and frontier verification use $A_r$.

Each follower also has local membership access to the finalized-identifier
dictionary $\mathsf{Done}_r$ maintained by the replicated state machine.  The
dictionary maps each finalized identifier to its committed round and fairness-
batch index; membership is the only operation needed during evidence replay.
Its authenticated root is
\[
 \mathsf{DoneRoot}_r=Commit(\mathsf{Done}_r;
                       \mathsf{AUTIG/finalized\mbox{-}set}).
\]
The dictionary may reuse the application's committed transaction index; it is
not an additional ordering graph.  A state checkpoint contains either its
canonical dictionary snapshot or an authenticated reference to the
corresponding application-state checkpoint, so a recovering replica can
answer membership queries for delayed log entries.

The follower state installed after committed fragment $r$ is
\[
 \Sigma_r=\langle r,\mathsf{epoch}_r,\beta_r,\mathsf{Seq}_r,
 \mathsf{Head}_r,\mathsf{Live}_r,\mathsf{TxRef}_r,\mathsf{Pos}_r,c_r,W_r,
 \mathsf{Done}_r,\mathsf{DoneRoot}_r,h_r\rangle.
 \tag{7}\label{eq:follower-state}
\]
$\beta_r$ is the largest fairness-batch index assigned through fragment $r$
(with $\beta_0=0$).  $c_r$, $W_r$, $S_r$, and $A_r$ are deterministic caches derived from
$\mathsf{Pos}_r$.  $\mathsf{TxRef}_r$ preserves the canonical content
commitment for each live identifier; retrievable bytes accompany checkpoints
through the data-availability rule.  Followers do not maintain the UTIG edge set $E$, SCCs, the
condensation graph, or a topological order; we refer to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:follower-state} as the \emph{authenticated graph-free follower
state}.

The predecessor and successor identifiers bind the deterministic transition
sequence, while checkpoint digests bind materialized state.  For an admitted
batch, finalized prefix, and certified SCC partition, define
\[
 \widehat h_r=\Commit(\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,h_{r-1},
                    H(\Enc(\mathcal L_r));
                    \mathsf{AUTIG/prestate}),
 \qquad
 h_r=\Commit(\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,\widehat h_r,F_r,\mathsf{Part}_r;
             \mathsf{AUTIG/poststate}).
 \tag{8}\label{eq:state-identifiers}
\]
Their security follows by induction from deterministic replay of the
underlying committed fragments; checkpoint snapshots receive a separate
content digest as described below.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Deterministic Evidence Transition and UTIG Refresh}
\label{sec:evidence-transition}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each fragment is processed in two stages:
\[
 \Sigma_{r-1}\xrightarrow{\;\mathcal L_r\;}
 \widehat\Sigma_r\xrightarrow{\;F_r\;}
 \Sigma_r.
 \tag{9}\label{eq:two-stage-transition}
\]
$\widehat\Sigma_r$ is a candidate pre-finalization state.  It is safe to
derive while verifying a proposal, whereas $\Sigma_r$ is installed only after
the underlying BFT commits the fragment.

\begin{algorithm}[h!]
\caption{\textsc{ApplyEvidence}: Deterministic Evidence Transition}
\label{alg:apply-evidence}
\footnotesize
\Input{Committed state $\Sigma_{r-1}$; candidate batch $\mathcal L_r$}
\Output{Pre-finalization state $\widehat\Sigma_r$; touched nodes
        $\mathsf{TouchedNodes}_r$; touched pairs
        $\mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r$, or reject}
\If{$\mathcal L_r$ does not contain exactly $n-f$ distinct admissible
    senders}{\KwRet reject}
$\widehat\Sigma_r\gets\Sigma_{r-1}$;
$\mathsf{TouchedNodes}_r\gets\varnothing$;
$\mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r\gets\varnothing$\;
\ForEach{$L_i^r\in\mathcal L_r$}{
  Verify its signature, epoch, round, predecessor position/head, contiguous
  extension, and within-extension uniqueness\;
  Verify digest-bound availability and application validity for every new
  non-finalized identifier; store its unique $\widehat{\mathsf{TxRef}}_r$ or
  require equality with the retained digest\;
  $K_i^-\gets\{u:\mathsf{Pos}_{r-1}(i,u)\ne\bot\}$;
  $N_i\gets\varnothing$\;
  \ForEach{$u$ at position $p$ in $\mathsf{chunk}_i^r$}{
    \eIf{$u\in\mathsf{Done}_{r-1}$}{
      Treat $u$ as continuity-only; do not reactivate it or add incident
      evidence\;
    }{
      \If{$\mathsf{Pos}_{r-1}(i,u)\ne\bot$}{\KwRet reject}
      $\widehat{\mathsf{Pos}}_r(i,u)\gets p$;
      $\widehat{\mathsf{Live}}_r.\textsc{add}(u)$;
      $\widehat c_r(u)\gets\widehat c_r(u)+1$;
      $\mathsf{TouchedNodes}_r.\textsc{add}(u)$;
      $N_i.\textsc{add}(u)$\;
    }
  }
  $K_i^+\gets K_i^-\cup N_i$\;
  \ForEach{$\{u,v\}\subseteq K_i^+$ with
           $\{u,v\}\not\subseteq K_i^-$}{
    Increment exactly one of $\widehat W_r(u,v)$ and $\widehat W_r(v,u)$
    according to $\widehat{\mathsf{Pos}}_r(i,\cdot)$; positions occupied by
    continuity-only identifiers do not affect the comparison\;
    $\mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r.\textsc{add}(\{u,v\})$\;
  }
  $\widehat{\mathsf{Seq}}_r[i]\gets\ell_i^+$;
  $\widehat{\mathsf{Head}}_r[i]\gets\eta_i^+$\;
}
\ForEach{$u\in\mathsf{TouchedNodes}_r$}{
  $old\gets S_{r-1}(u)$, using $\Blank$ for a new transaction\;
  $\widehat S_r(u)\gets\textsc{DetermineState}(\widehat c_r(u))$\;
  \If{$old=\Blank$ and $\widehat S_r(u)\ne\Blank$}{
    $\widehat A_r.\textsc{add}(u)$;
    Add every live pair incident to $u$ to $\mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r$\;
  }
}
\KwRet $(\widehat\Sigma_r,\mathsf{TouchedNodes}_r,
         \mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r)$
\end{algorithm}

The order leader applies Algorithm~\ref{alg:apply-evidence}
to the authoritative position state and mirrors the result in
$G_{\mathrm{utig}}$.  For every pair in $\mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r$ whose endpoints
are in $\widehat A_r$, it removes the cached orientation and re-evaluates
$\mathsf{EdgePred}$ using the exact new weights.  A pair is touched whenever a
new unique contribution changes either direction, not only when a direction
crosses $T_{\mathrm{edge}}$ for the first time.  This also captures later
relative-weight overtaking and tie-breaking changes.  Nodes absent from
$\mathcal L_r$ retain their positions, visibility, state, and incident
evidence.

For analysis, let $G_r^\star=(\widehat A_r,E_r^\star)$ denote the graph
obtained by evaluating $\mathsf{EdgePred}_r$ on every pair in the authoritative
pre-finalization state, and let $\widetilde G_r$ denote the leader's
incrementally materialized graph.  Algorithm~\ref{alg:apply-evidence} adds
every pair whose predicate can change to $\mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r$: a pair is
included when it receives a new directional contribution, and every live pair
incident to a newly active vertex is included when that vertex leaves
$\Blank$.  Re-evaluating exactly this set makes
$\widetilde G_r=G_r^\star$; Appendix~\ref{app:incremental-materialization}
formalizes the equivalence and its versioned-snapshot requirement.

$\textsc{Finalize}(\widehat\Sigma_r,F_r,\mathsf{Part}_r)$ is also deterministic.  It first
requires that $F_r$ contain distinct, active, non-finalized identifiers.  It
assigns consecutive fairness-batch indices to the certified SCCs, records the
round and index of each identifier in the finalized dictionary, and removes
those identifiers from
$\widehat{\mathsf{Live}}_r$, deletes their live-data references and entries from the live
$\widehat{\mathsf{Pos}}_r$ maps, and deletes only aggregate weights incident
to them.  It preserves $\mathsf{Seq}_r$, $\mathsf{Head}_r$, and every position
and weight whose endpoints are both unfinalized.  The resulting state stores
the updated counter $\beta_r$, queryable $\mathsf{Done}_r$, its commitment
$\mathsf{DoneRoot}_r$, and current transition identifier $h_r$.
Consequently, a later chain-valid chunk may still contain a finalized
identifier, but that identifier cannot regain a live position or contribute a
new pair vote.

Since $T_{\mathrm{edge}}=T_{\mathrm{non\mbox{-}blank}}$ and every pair vote
also supplies visibility for both endpoints,
\[
 \mathsf{EdgePred}_r(u\to v)\Longrightarrow u,v\in A_r.
 \tag{10}\label{eq:edge-active-closure}
\]
Thus $A_r$, rather than $B_r$, contains every live endpoint that can constrain
the current finalization decision.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Safe Finalization and Fairness Batches}
\label{sec:safe-extraction}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let $\widehat G_r$ be the graph induced by $\widehat A_r$ under
$\mathsf{EdgePred}$, and let
\[
 \mathsf{Solid}_r=\{u\in\widehat A_r:\widehat S_r(u)=\Solid\}.
\]
For $u\in\widehat A_r$, $\mathsf{Anc}_r(u)$ contains $u$ and every vertex
that reaches $u$ in $\widehat G_r$.  AUTIG finalizes the set
\[
 K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}
   =\{u\in\mathsf{Solid}_r:
          \mathsf{Anc}_r(u)\subseteq\mathsf{Solid}_r\}.
 \tag{11}\label{eq:safe-closure}
\]
This is the unique maximal subset of Solid transactions that is down-closed
in the complete live graph.  Equivalently, after condensing
$\widehat G_r$, an SCC is safe if every transaction in that SCC and every
transaction in each ancestor SCC is Solid.  Safety can be computed in one
topological pass: a component is unsafe if it contains a non-Solid vertex or
has an unsafe predecessor.  A Solid transaction with a Shaded ancestor
remains live until that ancestor becomes Solid.

The SCC boundaries define the fairness batches.  Let $\beta_{r-1}$ be the
largest index assigned by earlier committed fragments.  If
$C_1,\ldots,C_k$ are the SCCs of
$\widehat G_r[K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}]$ in topological order, every transaction in
$C_j$ receives
\[
 b(u)=\beta_{r-1}+j\quad\text{for every }u\in C_j,
 \qquad \beta_r=\beta_{r-1}+k.
\]
An empty prefix leaves $\beta_r=\beta_{r-1}$.  The fragment therefore carries
a sequence of fairness batches, rather than treating the entire fragment as
one batch.  For execution, AUTIG serializes the transactions within $C_j$ by
increasing transaction identifier; this serialization is deterministic but
does not change their common fairness-batch index.  Across SCCs, Kahn's
algorithm repeatedly selects the zero-indegree component with the smallest
$\mathsf{cid}(C)=\min\{\mathsf{txid}(u):u\in C\}$.  The public rules determine
both the batch sequence and its execution serialization.

The structural certificate is
\[
 \mathcal P_r=\langle\mathsf{Part}_r,\mathsf{InTree}_r,
 \mathsf{OutTree}_r,\mathsf{Rank}_r,\mathsf{BlockForest}_r\rangle.
 \tag{12}\label{eq:structural-proof}
\]
$\mathsf{Part}_r$ assigns each transaction in $F_r$ to a claimed SCC.  Two
spanning trees rooted at the smallest identifier certify strong connectivity
within each component, and $\mathsf{Rank}_r$ records the deterministic
condensation order.  $\mathsf{BlockForest}_r$ explains every excluded Solid
transaction.  Its roots are Shaded vertices in
$\widehat A_r\setminus K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$; each non-root record contains a
parent edge $p\to v$ and a depth one larger than its parent's depth.  Every
$s\in\mathsf{Solid}_r\setminus K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$ occurs in the forest and
traces to a Shaded root.  Hence each excluded Solid transaction has a
non-Solid ancestor.  Paths may share records, keeping the blocker certificate
linear in the number of represented vertices.  Pair weights are omitted from
$\mathcal P_r$ because followers derive them from $\widehat\Sigma_r$.

\begin{algorithm}[h!]
\caption{\textsc{SafeExtract}: Safe Extraction and Structural Certification}
\label{alg:safe-extract}
\footnotesize
\Input{Pre-finalization state $\widehat\Sigma_r$; materialized UTIG}
\Output{Serialized prefix $F_r$; fairness-batch partition
        $\mathsf{Part}_r$; certificate $\mathcal P_r$}
Build $\widehat G_r$ on $\widehat A_r$ from the cached UTIG edges\;
Compute its SCCs, condensation DAG, and $\mathsf{Solid}_r$\;
Mark an SCC unsafe if it contains a non-Solid vertex or has an unsafe
predecessor; let $K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$ be the union of unmarked SCCs\;
$\mathsf{Rank}_r\gets\textsc{DeterministicTopo}
  (\widehat G_r[K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}])$ using minimum-$\mathsf{cid}$
  Kahn tie-breaking\;
$F_r\gets$ the SCCs in increasing rank, with each SCC serialized by
increasing transaction identifier\;
Construct $\mathsf{Part}_r$ and two rooted spanning trees per included SCC\;
Run a multi-source search from Shaded vertices outside
$K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$ and retain parent/depth records that cover every
$s\in\mathsf{Solid}_r\setminus K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$\;
$\mathcal P_r\gets\langle\mathsf{Part}_r,\mathsf{InTree}_r,
 \mathsf{OutTree}_r,\mathsf{Rank}_r,\mathsf{BlockForest}_r\rangle$\;
\KwRet $(F_r,\mathsf{Part}_r,\mathcal P_r)$
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Running example.}
Consider $n=5$, $f=1$, and $\gamma=1$, which gives $q_h=4$,
$T_{\mathrm{edge}}=2$, and $T_{\mathrm{solid}}=3$.  Suppose the committed
prefixes currently expose
\[
 L_1=[e,a,b,c,d],\qquad
 L_2=[b,c,a,e,d],\qquad
 L_3=[c,a,b,d],
\]
while the remaining replicas have not committed positions for these
transactions.  Transactions $a,b,c,d$ are Solid and $e$ is Shaded.  The
derived edges contain the cycle $a\to b\to c\to a$, as well as
$a,b,c\to d$ and $e\to d$.  The SCC $\{a,b,c\}$ is safe, whereas $d$ is not:
its Shaded ancestor $e$ prevents $d$ from entering
$K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$.  The fragment therefore carries the single fairness
batch $\{a,b,c\}$, serialized as $[a,b,c]$.  The certificate contains two
trees for $\{a,b,c\}$, rank zero for that SCC, and the blocker edge
$e\to d$.  The follower accepts only if its local weights validate these
edges and no vertex outside $\{a,b,c\}$ has an incoming dependency into the
proposed batch.  After additional committed prefixes make $e$ Solid, the
blocker disappears and $d$ becomes eligible for a later safe closure.

\subsection{Proof-Carrying Fragment Verification}
\label{sec:proof-carrying-verification}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The leader proposes
\[
 \mathcal F_r=\langle\hdr_r,F_r,\mathcal L_r,\mathcal P_r\rangle,
\]
where
\[
 \hdr_r=\langle\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,\leaderpk,H_{\mathcal F,\prev},
 h_{r-1},\widehat h_r,h_r,H_{\mathcal F_r}\rangle
\]
and
\[
 H_{\mathcal F_r}=\Commit(\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,\leaderpk,
 H_{\mathcal F,\prev},h_{r-1},\widehat h_r,h_r,
 F_r,\mathcal L_r,\mathcal P_r;\domfrag).
 \tag{13}\label{eq:fragment}
\]
The digest therefore binds both state-transition identifiers, the complete
admitted evidence batch, the proposed order, the structural certificate, and
the predecessor fragment.  The BFT layer commits the full fragment or a
digest only after satisfying its data-availability rule.

\begin{algorithm}[h!]
\caption{\textsc{VerifyFragment}: Verify a Proof-Carrying Fragment}
\label{alg:verify-fragment}
\footnotesize
\Input{Committed state $\Sigma_{r-1}$; candidate fragment $\mathcal F_r$}
\Output{$(accept,\Sigma_r^{\mathsf{cand}},h_r)$ or reject}
Verify the order-leader signature, epoch, sequence, predecessor fragment,
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:fragment}\;
$(\widehat\Sigma_r,\mathsf{TouchedNodes}_r,
  \mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r)\gets
 \textsc{ApplyEvidence}(\Sigma_{r-1},\mathcal L_r)$; reject on failure\;
Verify $\widehat h_r$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:state-identifiers}\;
Derive $\widehat A_r$, $\widehat S_r$, and $\mathsf{Solid}_r$ locally\;
\If{$F_r$ contains a duplicate, a finalized identifier, or an identifier
    outside $\widehat A_r$}{\KwRet reject}
\If{$\mathrm{set}(F_r)\not\subseteq\mathsf{Solid}_r$}{\KwRet reject}
Require $\mathrm{keys}(\mathsf{Part}_r)=\mathrm{set}(F_r)$ and exact
tree/rank key coverage, including empty coverage when $F_r=\varnothing$\;
\If{$F_r\ne\varnothing$}{
  Reconstruct every internal edge on $F_r$ by applying
  $\mathsf{EdgePred}$ to the local $\widehat W_r$ values\;
  Verify both spanning trees of every claimed SCC and verify that every
  cross-SCC edge strictly increases $\mathsf{Rank}_r$\;
  Recompute the minimum-$\mathsf{cid}$ Kahn order of the certified
  condensation DAG and require it to equal $\mathsf{Rank}_r$\;
  Sort each claimed SCC by transaction identifier and require the
  concatenation in rank order to equal $F_r$\;
}
\ForEach{$(x,y)\in(\widehat A_r\setminus\mathrm{set}(F_r))
                   \times\mathrm{set}(F_r)$}{
  \If{$\mathsf{EdgePred}_r(x\to y)$ under local $\widehat W_r$}{\KwRet reject}
}
Check that every blocker-forest vertex is in
$\widehat A_r\setminus\mathrm{set}(F_r)$ and that every root is Shaded\;
For every non-root blocker record $(p,v,d)$, verify
$\mathsf{EdgePred}_r(p\to v)$ locally and require
$d(v)=d(p)+1$\;
Require every $s\in\mathsf{Solid}_r\setminus\mathrm{set}(F_r)$ to occur in
$\mathsf{BlockForest}_r$ and to reach a Shaded root by parent pointers\;
Reject blocker records that are not on a certified path to an excluded Solid
transaction\;
$\Sigma_r^{\mathsf{cand}}\gets
  \textsc{Finalize}(\widehat\Sigma_r,F_r,\mathsf{Part}_r)$\;
Verify $h_r=\Commit(\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,\widehat h_r,F_r,\mathsf{Part}_r;
                    \mathsf{AUTIG/poststate})$\;
\KwRet $(accept,\Sigma_r^{\mathsf{cand}},h_r)$
\end{algorithm}

The certificate proves both safety and maximal progress.  The two trees make
each claimed group strongly connected, while complete internal cross-group
inspection and increasing ranks establish SCC maximality.  The frontier scan
shows that the proposed set is down-closed, and the Solid check ensures that
every included transaction has sufficient committed visibility.  Conversely,
the blocker forest gives every excluded Solid transaction a Shaded ancestor.
These checks establish
$\mathrm{set}(F_r)=K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$.  Therefore an empty prefix is valid
exactly when $K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}=\varnothing$; the Solid set itself may be
non-empty.  Such a fragment can still commit $\mathcal L_r$ and advance the
evidence state.  The rank, SCC-boundary, and identifier checks uniquely
determine the fairness-batch sequence and its execution serialization.

Verification never mutates $\Sigma_{r-1}$.  A follower may retain
$\Sigma_r^{\mathsf{cand}}$ as speculative data, but it installs that state,
advances $h_r$, and releases $F_r$ for execution only after the underlying BFT
commits the corresponding $H_{\mathcal F_r}$.  Competing or equivocated
proposals derived from the same predecessor therefore cannot fork the
committed evidence state.

\subsection{Checkpointing, Pruning, Recovery, and Handoff}
\label{sec:checkpoint-recovery}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Committed live-evidence checkpoints.}
Every $K$ committed fragments, replicas canonically encode a snapshot
\[
 \mathsf{Snap}_r=\Enc(\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,\beta_r,\mathsf{Seq}_r,
 \mathsf{Head}_r,\mathsf{Live}_r,\mathsf{TxRef}_r,\mathsf{Pos}_r,
 \mathsf{Done}_r,\mathsf{DoneRoot}_r,h_r)
\]
and compute $d_r=H(\mathsf{Snap}_r)$.  A checkpoint is
\[
 \mathsf{CP}_r=\langle\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,h_r,d_r,
                       \mathsf{AvailQC}_r\rangle,
 \tag{14}\label{eq:checkpoint}
\]
where every acknowledgement in $\mathsf{AvailQC}_r$ signs the exact tuple
$(\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,h_r,d_r)$ and the certificate contains $n-f$ distinct
signers.  A correct replica acknowledges only the digest obtained from its
local committed state and only after it can serve the snapshot and every live
transaction named by $\mathsf{TxRef}_r$.  Snapshot encoding is canonical, and
a correct availability signer retains this recovery package until a successor
checkpoint is committed and available.
An $n-f$-signature certificate contains at least $n-2f$ correct holders;
under the retention and eventual-communication assumptions, at least one
digest-matching snapshot remains retrievable.  The BFT
data-availability layer retains every committed post-checkpoint fragment,
including its complete admitted LocalOrder chunks and finalized $F_r$, needed
for replay.

A recovering replica obtains a snapshot from any holder whose bytes hash to
$d_r$, resolves and validates every live-data reference, derives $W_r$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:position-weight}, and replays only
BFT-committed fragments after $r$.  It never replays merely received
proposals.  An implementation may let a holder return $W_r$ as an optional
auxiliary cache alongside the digest-bound snapshot, but recovery must
validate every cached value against the authoritative positions before using
it.  Deterministic application and
finalization yield the same current state as uninterrupted execution.  A new
order leader then
materializes $S$, $A$, $E$, SCC metadata, and other UTIG caches from that
recovered evidence state.  In particular, external--external evidence is not
restored from zero and is not inferred from the last finalized prefix.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:recover-state} specifies the validation and replay order;
Appendix~\ref{app:checkpoint-handoff} proves that the recovered state is exact,
rather than a conservative approximation.

\paragraph{Soft and hard pruning.}
Soft pruning removes only derived leader-side structures: cached edges,
adjacency indexes, SCC/condensation metadata, topological ranks, and other
reconstructible acceleration data.  It retains the authoritative live
positions and all aggregate weights between live transactions.  A follower may
likewise evict a derived weight cache only while retaining the positions needed
to recompute it.  Cache age or last-seen metadata may guide this performance
policy but does not enter any acceptance predicate.
Theorem~\ref{thm:pruning_safety} shows that rematerialization from the retained
evidence produces exactly the unpruned extraction view.  Hard deletion is
limited to evidence incident to a transaction after its inclusion in a
BFT-committed $F_r$; the finalized-identifier index prevents reactivation.
Evidence between two unfinalized transactions is never hard-deleted merely
because it is old or currently outside an extraction prefix.
Theorem~\ref{thm:hard-deletion} establishes that the committed safe closure
already contains every fairness-relevant predecessor of a finalized
transaction, which justifies deleting finalized-incident live evidence.
Appendix~\ref{app:pruning-retention} states the retained-state boundary and
shows why neither cache eviction nor delayed log delivery changes future
acceptance decisions.

\paragraph{Objective faults and missing proposals.}
Objective invalidity and silence use different handoff evidence.  A
leader-signed fragment that fails a deterministic public check is a candidate
$\mathsf{OrderFault}$ proof by itself; any replica may submit the signed
fragment and failing predicate to the underlying BFT, whose replicas verify
and order the replacement decision.  Safety does not depend on collecting
$f+1$ complaints for the same digest, because a Byzantine order leader may
equivocate with different invalid fragments.  In contrast, non-receipt is not
publicly verifiable.  After a locally increasing timeout, replicas sign
$\mathsf{OrderTimeout}(\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,h_{r-1})$; $n-f$ matching timeout
signatures form a handoff certificate.  Once a timeout/handoff certificate for
$(\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,h_{r-1})$ is committed by the underlying BFT, any late
fragment from the replaced order leader for that tuple is inadmissible.  The
successor resumes from the latest committed state/checkpoint, never from an
uncommitted candidate.

\section{Security Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AUTIG maintains five invariants across committed fragments: each replica log
extends one committed head; each replica contributes at most one direction to
each live pair; correct followers derive the same evidence state; uncommitted
candidate states cannot affect subsequent verification; and evidence between
two unfinalized transactions survives finalization, checkpointing, and
handoff.  We establish fairness in three steps.  First, prefix completeness
turns honest receive-order observations into committed pair support.  Second,
the threshold condition makes that support both sufficient and strictly
dominant whenever the later transaction is Solid.  Third, safe-closure
verification places every required predecessor in the same or an earlier
fairness batch.

\begin{theorem}[Authenticated-state agreement]
\label{thm:state-agreement}
Correct replicas that commit the same fragment sequence derive identical
$\Sigma_r$, $h_r$, active sets, transaction states, and pair weights.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Incremental-materialization equivalence]
\label{thm:incremental-equivalence}
Suppose the order leader starts round $r$ with a materialized graph equal to
the authoritative graph induced by $\Sigma_{r-1}$.  After applying
Algorithm~\ref{alg:apply-evidence}, re-evaluating
$\mathsf{EdgePred}_r$ for exactly $\mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r$, and removing the
vertices finalized by a committed fragment, its persistent UTIG equals the
graph reconstructed from the resulting authoritative follower state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Safe-fragment soundness]
\label{thm:safe-fragment}
If Algorithm~\ref{alg:verify-fragment} accepts $\mathcal F_r$, then
$\mathrm{set}(F_r)=K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$.  Its partition is the SCC partition
of $\widehat G_r[K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}]$, and its ranks and execution order equal
the public deterministic linearization.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[$\gamma$-Batch-Order-Fairness]
\label{thm:batch-fairness}
Assume Eq.~\eqref{eq:integer-feasibility}.  For distinct valid transactions
$x$ and $y$ that are eventually received by all correct replicas, if at least
$q_h$ honest replicas receive $x$ before $y$, then every committed AUTIG
execution satisfies $b(x)\le b(y)$ whenever $y$ is finalized.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Soft-pruning equivalence]
\label{thm:pruning_safety}
Let an unpruned leader and a soft-pruned leader start from the same committed
evidence state $\Sigma_r$.  The soft-pruned leader may evict any derived UTIG
cache, including edges, adjacency indexes, SCC metadata, condensation ranks,
and topological order, but retains every authoritative position and every
live--live pair value.  Rematerializing the evicted caches yields the same
$\widehat G_r$, $K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$, fairness-batch partition, and accepted
fragment as the unpruned execution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Hard-deletion safety]
\label{thm:hard-deletion}
After a committed fragment finalizes $u$, AUTIG may delete live positions and
pair weights incident to $u$ without changing the fairness-batch assignment
of any unfinalized transaction or invalidating a future fairness obligation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Recovery equivalence]
\label{thm:recovery}
A replica that restores a digest-matching committed checkpoint and replays the
subsequent BFT-committed fragments derives the same evidence state as an
uninterrupted correct replica.
\end{theorem}

The proof intuition is as follows.  When $y$ is finalized, it is Solid.  If
the fairness antecedent holds and $x$ is still live, the quorum-intersection
bound establishes a strict edge $x\to y$.  Since the accepted prefix is
down-closed, $x$ belongs to the same prefix; SCC membership then assigns $x$
to either the same fairness batch as $y$ or an earlier one.  The same argument
shows that no late chunk can reveal a previously omitted fairness predecessor
of a finalized transaction, which permits finalized-incident deletion.
Checkpoint equivalence follows by induction over the deterministic evidence
transition.  Appendix~\ref{app:autig-security-details} gives the complete
proofs.

\begin{proposition}[Progress under stabilized ancestry]
\label{prop:stabilized-progress}
Assume eventual transaction dissemination, eventual admission of every
pending honest log position, and an honest order leader after GST.  Let $u$ be
a valid live transaction whose resulting finite ancestor set also satisfies
the dissemination premise.  If that ancestor set stops expanding, then $u$ is
eventually included in a committed safe closure.
\end{proposition}

Every transaction in the resulting finite ancestor set eventually obtains at
least $n-f$ correct positions and therefore becomes Solid.  Once this occurs,
the entire ancestor set is safe and maximal-progress verification requires its
inclusion.  The stabilized-ancestry premise is necessary for this extraction
rule: unrestricted creation of new Shaded ancestors can extend a dependency
chain indefinitely.  Section~\ref{sec:discussion-scope} states the resulting
liveness scope.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sparse positions require $O(n|\mathsf{Live}_r|)$ entries, and the aggregate
weight cache requires $O(|\mathsf{Live}_r|^2)$ counters in the worst case.  If
$N_i$ contains the new positions of replica $i$, evidence application touches
$O(|N_i||K_i^-|+|N_i|^2)$ pairs; no round-wide transaction-state scan is
required.  The SCC trees and ranks contain $O(|F_r|)$ records.  A blocker
forest contains $O(|V_{\mathsf{blk}}|)$ records, where
$|V_{\mathsf{blk}}|\le|A_r\setminus F_r|$; it is empty when every Solid
transaction is safe.  Verification reads
\[
 \frac{|F_r|(|F_r|-1)}{2}
 +( |A_r|-|F_r| )|F_r|+O(|V_{\mathsf{blk}}|)
\]
local pair values.  Thus, worst-case verification remains
$\Theta(|A_r|^2)$, although followers avoid persistent edge materialization,
SCC maintenance, and full-live-graph extraction.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Discussion and Scope}
\label{sec:discussion-scope}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AUTIG replicates authenticated receipt-order evidence at followers and does
not eliminate evidence-state maintenance.  Its structural certificate is
linear in the represented finalization and blocker subgraphs, while a verifier
may still inspect quadratically many local pair values in the worst case.  The
fairness definition assigns one batch index to every SCC; deterministic
serialization removes leader choice within that SCC but does not provide a
stronger intra-batch fairness guarantee.  The safe-closure rule provides
batch-order-fairness; under continuous arrivals, it provides progress once a
transaction's ancestry stabilizes.  Expiring an incoming edge solely because
its destination is old would define a different, bounded-fairness property: the
discarded edge may still be required by the receive-order antecedent.  Preserving
the stated fairness notion while obtaining Themis-style standard liveness
requires committed partial ordering frames and batch unspooling, so that each
frame has finite membership while unresolved Shaded--Shaded relations are
completed by later evidence.  The protocol analyzed here retains safe closure
and consequently establishes the stabilized-ancestry progress property of
Proposition~\ref{prop:stabilized-progress}.

\section{Detailed Security Proofs}
\label{app:autig-security-details}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proofs below use the candidate pre-finalization state
$\widehat\Sigma_r$; hats are omitted where the round is clear.

\begin{lemma}[Position uniqueness and deterministic weights]
\label{lem:position-uniqueness}
For any live transaction $u$, replica $i$ has at most one
$\mathsf{Pos}_r(i,u)$.  For every unordered live pair $\{u,v\}$, the two
weights are uniquely determined by Eq.~\eqref{eq:position-weight}, and one
replica contributes to at most one direction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An admitted extension starts at the committed sequence number and log head,
and positions inside the extension are contiguous.  Algorithm~\ref{alg:apply-evidence}
rejects a second live position for the same sender and identifier.  The two
indicators for $u\prec v$ and $v\prec u$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:position-weight}
are mutually exclusive, so the committed positions uniquely determine both
weights.  Deterministic replay proves Theorem~\ref{thm:state-agreement} by
induction on the committed fragment sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Committed observation coverage]
\label{lem:committed-observation-coverage}
Let $H_{xy}$ contain at least $q_h$ honest replicas that received $x$ before
$y$, and suppose $x$ is not finalized in pre-state $\Sigma_r$.  Define
\[
 Y_r(y)=\{i:\mathsf{Pos}_r(i,y)\ne\bot\},
 \qquad c_r(y)=|Y_r(y)|.
\]
Then
\[
 W_r(x,y)\ge L_r(x,y)
   =\max\{0,q_h+c_r(y)-n\}.
 \tag{15}\label{eq:coverage-lower-bound}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two sets are drawn from $n$ replicas, so
$|H_{xy}\cap Y_r(y)|\ge q_h+c_r(y)-n$.  Every replica in the intersection is
honest and has committed a prefix containing $y$.  Prefix completeness and
the assumption that $x$ remains unfinalized imply that the same prefix contains
$x$ at a smaller position.  Each intersection member contributes one unit to
$W_r(x,y)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fair edge formation]
\label{lem:fair-edge-formation}
Under Eq.~\eqref{eq:integer-feasibility}, if the premises of
Lemma~\ref{lem:committed-observation-coverage} hold and $y$ is Solid, then
$W_r(x,y)\ge T_{\mathrm{edge}}$ and
$W_r(x,y)>W_r(y,x)$.  Hence $\mathsf{EdgePred}_r(x\to y)$ holds independently
of deterministic tie-breaking.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $c=c_r(y)$ and $L=q_h+c-n$.  Solidity gives $c\ge n-2f$, and therefore
\[
 L\ge q_h-2f\ge n-q_h+1=T_{\mathrm{edge}},
\]
where the second inequality is Eq.~\eqref{eq:integer-feasibility}.  Every
contributor to $W_r(y,x)$ belongs to $Y_r(y)$, whereas the $L$ contributors
identified above cannot contribute the reverse direction.  Thus
$W_r(y,x)\le c-L$.  Finally,
\[
 2L-c\ge2q_h+c-2n\ge2q_h-n-2f\ge1,
\]
which proves $W_r(x,y)\ge L>c-L\ge W_r(y,x)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Safe-closure certificate]
\label{lem:safe-closure-certificate}
If Algorithm~\ref{alg:verify-fragment} accepts the structural certificate and
frontier scan, then $\mathrm{set}(F_r)=K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$ and the claimed
groups are the maximal SCCs of the induced graph.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The inward and outward trees make every claimed component strongly connected.
Complete internal cross-component inspection and strictly increasing ranks
rule out a cycle among claimed components, establishing SCC maximality.  Every
included vertex is Solid.  The frontier scan rules out an edge from outside
$F_r$ into $F_r$; any outside ancestor path would contain such a first entering
edge.  Hence every ancestor of an included vertex is also included and Solid,
which proves $F_r\subseteq K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$.  Conversely, each excluded
Solid vertex has a verified parent chain from a Shaded root and therefore has
a non-Solid ancestor.  No excluded Solid vertex belongs to
$K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$, proving the reverse inclusion.  Recomputing the public
tie-breaking rules proves the remaining claims of
Theorem~\ref{thm:safe-fragment}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:batch-fairness}]
Consider the committed fragment that first finalizes $y$.  If $x$ was
finalized earlier, then $b(x)<b(y)$.  Otherwise,
Lemma~\ref{lem:fair-edge-formation} gives $x\to y$ in the candidate live
graph.  Theorem~\ref{thm:safe-fragment} makes $F_r$ down-closed, so
$x\in F_r$.  If $x$ and $y$ share an SCC, they receive the same fairness-batch
index; otherwise the condensation order places $x$ in an earlier batch.
Thus $b(x)\le b(y)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pruning_safety}]
The authoritative committed positions uniquely determine visibility counts,
transaction states, the active set, and both directional weights for every
live pair.  Applying $\mathsf{EdgePred}$ to these values therefore reconstructs
exactly the edge set of the unpruned UTIG.  SCC decomposition, condensation,
safe-set marking, and deterministic linearization are pure functions of this
edge set.  Consequently, cache eviction changes neither the rematerialized
graph nor $K_r^{\mathsf{safe}}$.  Algorithm~\ref{alg:verify-fragment} checks the
resulting certificate against the same authoritative follower state, so both
executions produce the same acceptable fragment.  The argument does not apply
to deletion of positions or live--live evidence, which soft pruning forbids.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:hard-deletion}]
Let $u$ be finalized in round $r$.  For any transaction $x$ whose honest
receive order satisfies the fairness antecedent $x\prec u$, the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:batch-fairness} shows that $x$ is finalized in the same or
an earlier fairness batch.  Therefore a later chunk cannot reveal an
unfinalized fairness predecessor of $u$.  Removing positions and weights
incident to $u$ does not change any pair weight whose two endpoints remain
live, and $\mathsf{Done}_r$ prevents a delayed occurrence of $u$ from
reactivating it.  Future batch assignments among live transactions are
unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:recovery}]
The checkpoint digest binds the canonical live-data references, positions, log
heads, and finalized dictionary.  After the referenced data are retrieved and
validated, these values uniquely derive support counts, states,
the active set, and pair weights.  Starting from this common base, each replayed
fragment has one deterministic evidence transition and one deterministic
finalization transition.  Induction over the BFT-committed suffix yields the
same $\Sigma_r$ and $h_r$ as uninterrupted execution.
\end{proof}

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Incremental UTIG Materialization}
\label{app:incremental-materialization}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The authoritative graph for candidate state $\widehat\Sigma_r$ is
$G_r^\star=(\widehat A_r,E_r^\star)$, where
$(u,v)\in E_r^\star$ exactly when
$\mathsf{EdgePred}_r(u\to v)$ evaluates to true using the positions and
weights derived from $\widehat\Sigma_r$.  The materialized graph
$\widetilde G_r$ is a leader-side cache; it is not an additional source of
protocol truth.

\begin{lemma}[Touched-pair completeness]
\label{lem:touched-pair-completeness}
For every unordered pair $\{u,v\}\subseteq\widehat A_r$, if its membership or
orientation in the authoritative graph can differ after
Algorithm~\ref{alg:apply-evidence}, then
$\{u,v\}\in\mathsf{TouchedPairs}_r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
There are two cases.  First, if both endpoints were active before the
transition, an edge predicate can change only if a new committed position
changes $W(u,v)$ or $W(v,u)$.  The pair-update loop adds every pair receiving
such a first contribution, including changes that preserve the same threshold
side but alter relative support.  Second, suppose at least one endpoint was
not previously active.  The endpoint can enter $\widehat A_r$ only by receiving
a new committed position that moves it from $\Blank$ to a non-Blank state.
The state-update loop then adds every live incident pair.  If neither case
applies, the active membership and both directional weights are unchanged, so
the deterministic edge predicate is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:incremental-equivalence}]
Assume inductively that the persistent UTIG before round $r$ equals the graph
derived from $\Sigma_{r-1}$.  Algorithm~\ref{alg:apply-evidence} does not modify
an authoritative position except by assigning a previously absent first
position.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:touched-pair-completeness}, every edge whose
existence or orientation can change is re-evaluated, while every untouched
edge has the same endpoints and predicate inputs as before.  Adding newly
active vertices and applying the refreshed predicates therefore yields
$\widetilde G_r=G_r^\star$ before extraction.  Once the fragment commits,
finalization deletes exactly the vertices in $F_r$ and their incident edges.
It does not change a position or pair value whose endpoints both remain live.
The post-finalization UTIG consequently equals the authoritative graph derived
from $\Sigma_r$, completing the induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Version-consistent extraction]
\label{lem:version-consistent-extraction}
If graph refresh, safe extraction, and certificate construction read one
immutable evidence version identified by $\widehat h_r$, then their output is
equivalent to a serial execution of Algorithms~\ref{alg:apply-evidence} and
\ref{alg:safe-extract} on $\widehat\Sigma_r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The immutable version fixes the active set, every pair value, and therefore
the complete authoritative graph.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:incremental-equivalence}, the refreshed UTIG represents that
same graph.  SCC decomposition, safe-set marking, and deterministic
linearization are pure functions of this version.  Equation~\eqref{eq:fragment}
binds $\widehat h_r$ to the certificate and proposed prefix, and
Algorithm~\ref{alg:verify-fragment} reconstructs the same evidence version
before checking them.  Thus, an update to a later evidence version cannot be
observed partially by an accepted fragment.  An implementation may realize
this requirement with immutable snapshots, copy-on-write versions, or an
equivalent single-writer/multiple-reader protocol.
\end{proof}

\section{Pruning and State Retention}
\label{app:pruning-retention}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AUTIG distinguishes authoritative evidence from reconstructible graph state.
The authoritative state consists of the per-replica log heads and sequence
numbers, live-data references, live first positions, finalized-identifier
dictionary, fairness-batch counter, and committed transition identifier.
Visibility counts, transaction
states, aggregate weights, active-set indexes, dependency edges, SCCs, and
condensation metadata are deterministic derivatives.  This distinction gives
the following retention rules.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Edges, adjacency indexes, SCCs, condensation ranks, and extraction
        workspaces may be evicted at any time and reconstructed from the
        authenticated evidence state.
  \item A cached aggregate weight may be evicted only while the live positions
        from which it is derived remain available.
  \item Live-data references, positions, and aggregate weights incident to $u$
        may be deleted only
        after a BFT-committed fragment finalizes $u$.  The committed log head
        and sequence number remain, and $u$ remains queryable through
        $\mathsf{Done}$.
  \item Evidence for a pair whose two endpoints remain live is retained,
        independently of age, current state, or distance from the extracted
        prefix.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}[Delayed-extension compatibility]
\label{lem:delayed-extension-compatibility}
Deleting live positions incident to a finalized transaction does not prevent
the admission of a later prefix-consistent LocalOrder that contains that
identifier.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Admissibility checks the extension's predecessor sequence number and hash head,
neither of which is deleted by finalization.  An occurrence found in
$\mathsf{Done}$ is consumed for log continuity but is not assigned a new live
position and contributes no incident weight.  Every non-finalized identifier
later in the same contiguous chunk is processed at its absolute log position.
Consequently, delayed delivery advances the replica log without reactivating
the finalized transaction or changing a live--live comparison.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:pruning_safety} follows because soft pruning removes only
deterministic derivatives.  Theorem~\ref{thm:hard-deletion} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:delayed-extension-compatibility} establish the stronger boundary
for committed finalized identifiers.  In contrast, deleting a live--live
position can change a cumulative weight, an edge predicate, and a future safe
closure; such a deletion is not a semantics-preserving pruning operation.
The state size is therefore bounded by the currently retained live evidence
and finalized-membership representation, rather than by a constant independent
of workload.

\section{Checkpoint Recovery and Order-Leader Handoff}
\label{app:checkpoint-handoff}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recovery begins from the latest checkpoint whose identifier is committed by
the hosting BFT.  The snapshot reconstructs the complete authoritative
evidence state at that checkpoint; the committed suffix supplies every later
evidence and finalization transition.

\begin{algorithm}[h!]
\caption{\textsc{RecoverState}: Checkpoint Recovery and Committed Replay}
\label{alg:recover-state}
\footnotesize
\Input{Committed checkpoint $\mathsf{CP}_j$; candidate snapshot
       $\mathsf{Snap}_j$; BFT-committed fragment suffix
       $\mathcal C_{j+1:r}$}
\Output{Recovered committed state $\Sigma_r$, or reject}
Verify that $\mathsf{CP}_j$ is BFT-committed and contains $n-f$ valid
availability signatures on $(\mathsf{epoch}_j,j,h_j,d_j)$\;
Require canonical snapshot encoding, $H(\mathsf{Snap}_j)=d_j$, and equality of
its epoch, round, and transition identifier with $\mathsf{CP}_j$\;
Decode $\mathsf{Seq}_j$, $\mathsf{Head}_j$, $\mathsf{Live}_j$,
$\mathsf{TxRef}_j$, $\mathsf{Pos}_j$, $\mathsf{Done}_j$,
$\mathsf{DoneRoot}_j$, and $\beta_j$\;
Verify $\mathsf{Live}_j\cap\mathsf{Done}_j=\varnothing$,
$\mathrm{dom}(\mathsf{TxRef}_j)=\mathsf{Live}_j$,
$\mathsf{DoneRoot}_j=\Commit(\mathsf{Done}_j;
\mathsf{AUTIG/finalized\mbox{-}set})$, and that every stored position is
consistent with its sender's committed sequence number\;
Resolve every $\mathsf{TxRef}_j[u]$, require its transaction digest to equal
$u$, and verify the deterministic application-validity predicate\;
Derive $c_j$, $S_j$, $A_j$, and every required $W_j$ from
$\mathsf{Pos}_j$; validate and discard any supplied derived cache\;
$\Sigma\gets\Sigma_j$ reconstructed from the verified snapshot\;
\ForEach{$\mathcal F_k$ in the BFT-committed order $k=j+1,\ldots,r$}{
  Require the committed digest and available fragment bytes to match\;
  $(accept,\Sigma^{\mathsf{cand}},h_k)\gets
     \textsc{VerifyFragment}(\Sigma,\mathcal F_k)$\;
  \If{verification rejects}{\KwRet reject}
  $\Sigma\gets\Sigma^{\mathsf{cand}}$\;
}
\KwRet $\Sigma$
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Snapshot availability.}
Every $n-f$-signature $\mathsf{AvailQC}_j$ contains at least $n-2f$ correct
signers.  Each correct signer attests to the complete tuple
$(\mathsf{epoch}_j,j,h_j,d_j)$ and retains the matching snapshot and referenced
live data until a successor checkpoint becomes committed and available.  Under
eventual communication,
at least one such holder supplies a digest-matching snapshot.  Collision
resistance prevents a different snapshot from satisfying the committed
digest.

\paragraph{Exact reconstruction.}
The snapshot fixes the authoritative base state.  Equation~\eqref{eq:position-weight}
then fixes every cumulative pair value, including external--external pairs
that no finalized prefix would reveal.  Each suffix fragment contains the
complete admitted LocalOrders and finalization data required by
Algorithm~\ref{alg:verify-fragment}; replay excludes uncommitted proposals.
The induction in Theorem~\ref{thm:recovery} therefore yields the exact current
state, rather than a lower bound or conservative superset of historical
evidence.

\begin{corollary}[Order-leader handoff continuity]
\label{cor:handoff-continuity}
An order leader that starts from the state returned by
Algorithm~\ref{alg:recover-state} materializes the same current UTIG and
extracts the same safe closure as an uninterrupted correct order leader.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:recovery} gives equality of the authoritative follower state,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:incremental-equivalence} makes the materialized UTIG a
deterministic derivative of that state.  The successor ignores uncommitted
candidate states.  If the hosting BFT has committed a timeout/handoff
certificate for $(\mathsf{epoch}_r,r,h_{r-1})$, fragments subsequently received
from the replaced leader for that tuple fail the epoch/sequence admissibility
check and cannot alter the recovered history.
\end{proof}
